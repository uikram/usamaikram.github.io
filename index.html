Usama Ikram

I am a graduate researcher in artificial intelligence, working in machine learning and computer vision with a growing focus on multimodal and foundation models. My research asks what happens to large pretrained models when they leave the benchmark: how they can be adapted to specialised domains at reasonable cost, and what determines whether the resulting systems are dependable enough to be used for the purpose they were built for.

That question has taken me across several settings. I have studied whether vision-language models can operate inside a real-time human-robot interaction loop, adapted lightweight vision backbones to biomedical imaging where the usual training objectives quietly fail, and led a systematic review that measured how much evidence actually stands behind a set of clinical claims. Before starting my MS I spent two years as an AI research engineer, building models that had to run on constrained hardware and on data that rarely behaved as documented. Much of what I now find interesting came from that period.

I have three first-authored manuscripts under review. Looking ahead, I want to work further into multimodal and foundation-model systems, efficient and deployable AI, and healthcare applications, and to develop that work alongside people who have been doing it longer than I have.

Research interests

Broadly, machine learning and computer vision, with particular interest in the areas below. Some of these I have worked in directly; others are directions I want to develop into during a PhD, and I have tried to keep that distinction visible.

Multimodal and foundation models

Vision-language models are where my hands-on experience sits, mainly on the adaptation and deployment side. I would like to move further into multimodal systems that combine vision, language and other modalities, and into how large generative models can support systems that cannot tolerate their latency.

Efficient and parameter-efficient learning

Low-rank adaptation of pretrained backbones, distilled and lightweight architectures, and the question of what a restricted adaptation subspace changes about what a model retains. In one of my studies it preserved rare classes that full fine-tuning destroyed, in a setting where it offered no compute advantage at all.

Real-time and deployable AI

What models cost to run, and what that cost implies for the systems around them. This includes latency and energy characterisation, graph compilation and quantisation, embedded inference, and treating operational requirements as part of the evaluation rather than an afterthought.

Healthcare AI and Healthcare 4.0

Most of my applied work has been medical or biological. The constraints there are distinctive: scarce labelled data, classes that are rare but clinically decisive, deployment environments without reliable compute, and users who need to know how much confidence a prediction carries.

Computer vision and biomedical image analysis

Segmentation, detection, and image analysis where the output feeds a downstream decision or measurement rather than being the end product, including quantitative imaging and holographic microscopy.

Evaluation and research practice

How confidently a reported result can be held. This came out of leading a systematic review, and it has changed how I read quantitative claims, including my own.

Delay-Aware Deployability of Multimodal Foundation Models for Real-Time Human-Robot Interaction

Usama Ikram, Youhyun Kim, Inkyu Moon

Submitted to IEEE Transactions on Human-Machine Systems Under review

Vision-language models are increasingly proposed as perception modules for embodied and assistive systems, and are compared almost entirely on task accuracy. In a wearable interaction setting that comparison is incomplete: the model's inference time enters the control loop as a transport delay, and past some threshold the loop becomes unstable however accurate the perception is. No accuracy benchmark locates that threshold, so this work derives it. Modelling the loop as a PID-controlled mass-spring-damper plant with perception delay, a first-order Padé approximation and the Routh-Hurwitz criterion give a stability bound of 37.77 ms, against which three architectures were evaluated on worst-case rather than mean latency.

  • LoRA adaptation of CLIP preserved representation quality (linear-probe average 85.69% against 85.95% for the baseline) while improving zero-shot transfer on remote-sensing imagery by 6.05%.
  • Merged LoRA-CLIP reached 17.92 ms worst-case latency, inside the nominal bound and inside the tighter 18.76 ms bound that survives ±50% variation in biomechanical parameters.
  • A Frozen Prefix-LM reached 442.02 ms end to end, 11.7× over the bound, despite having the fastest visual encoder of the three at 8.25 ms. The cost is autoregressive decoding rather than perception, which is a statement about interface design more than model capability.
  • Software-in-the-loop simulation stayed bounded at 30 ms and diverged at 40 ms, only 2.23 ms past the analytical bound, so the margin is tight rather than conservative.
  • The resulting recommendation is architectural: parameter-efficient multimodal models inside the high-rate perceptual loop, generative models in asynchronous supervisory roles.

Latency and energy were measured on a desktop RTX A5000; the edge and battery-life figures in the paper are labelled as projections. The Padé approximation makes the analytical bound mildly optimistic, which is why the safety margins and the simulation check are included.

Physics-Aware LoRA-Adapted Lightweight AI for Edge-Efficient Quantitative Holographic Cell Analysis

Usama Ikram, Inkyu Moon

Submitted to Applied Physics B: Lasers and Optics Under review

Label-free microscopy supports direct biophysical measurement of individual cells, which makes it attractive where staining is impractical. The learning problem is awkward in two ways: the models worth deploying are pretrained on natural images and transfer poorly, and standard segmentation losses compare pixel masks while the predicted boundary determines a downstream physical measurement the objective never constrains. A conventionally trained model in our ablation scored a competitive boundary F1 of 0.938 while misestimating the measured quantity by 5.22%. This work adds three domain-specific terms to the objective and studies how lightweight backbones should be adapted to the domain.

  • The full objective reduced measurement error from 5.22% to 3.04% on EdgeSAM and from 15.52% to 3.01% on MobileNet-UNet, with the best boundary fidelity of any configuration tested.
  • The terms have to be composed in order rather than added independently: applied without a geometric anchor, the contrast term collapsed boundary F1 to 0.386 and eliminated the rarest class outright.
  • Full fine-tuning eliminated a diagnostically informative minority class in all three backbones, while LoRA at rank 8 retained all four classes in both attention-based backbones. Since latency and memory were flat across ranks, the low-rank constraint is acting as a regulariser rather than a compute saving, which I think is the more interesting result.
  • Across 1,974 individually segmented cells in an 11-timepoint study, the framework recovered the expected biological trajectory and agreed with annotator-derived measurements at r = 0.966.
  • ONNX FP16 compilation brought the selected configuration to 4.33 ms median latency (228 FPS) at 16.4 MB peak allocated memory.

Efficiency figures are desktop RTX A5000 measurements; embedded deployment is motivated but not demonstrated. Absolute values of the derived measurement are calibrated against a population anchor because the archived data did not record its scale, so relative results are scale-invariant and absolute ones are not.

Determinants of Glycaemic Outcomes in Children and Adolescents with Type 1 Diabetes: A Systematic Review and Evidence Map

Usama Ikram (corresponding author), Abey Jose, Francisca C. Eyzaguirre, Alejandro Mac Cawley

Submitted to Diabetologia · PROSPERO CRD420251045872 Under review

Conducted with collaborators at Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile and the University of Limerick. Reviews in this area list determinants that have been shown to matter without recording how many studies tested each, so a factor with twenty replications and one resting on a single exploratory analysis carry equal weight. I led a review of 63 quantitative studies drawn from 5,406 records across four databases, screened in duplicate and reported to PRISMA 2020, and built the PEARL framework: 47 determinants across 12 sub-domains and five domains, assigned under four stated rules, with a counted weight of evidence attached to every category.

  • Evidence volume and consistency ran in opposite directions. The physiological domain holds roughly half of the 297 factor-study associations but only 68% of them reach significance, while all 26 associations in the psychological domain are significant.
  • A literature reporting no null result across 26 tests is unlikely to be the complete set of analyses performed, and selective reporting is a more economical explanation than uniformly larger effects.
  • Frequently cited determinants are not always well supported: sex reached significance in 9 of 23 studies, body mass index in 7 of 15.

Current work

End-to-end lightweight holographic analysis Ongoing, no results yet

The conventional pipeline performs reconstruction, segmentation and measurement as separate stages, each optimised for its own criterion, with the consequence that the reconstruction stage is tuned to produce an image that looks correct rather than one that supports the analysis performed on it. This project develops a single model that maps a raw hologram directly to both a reconstructed image and a segmentation map, and derives per-cell measurements from those two outputs.

  • Central comparison. Two acquisition configurations, off-axis and in-line Gabor holograms, evaluated across reconstruction accuracy, segmentation quality, morphology classification, measurement accuracy and computational cost. In-line Gabor is considerably simpler and cheaper to acquire but harder to invert, so establishing what it actually costs downstream is the practical result worth having.
  • Method. Extending my earlier objective from segmentation alone to a joint criterion over reconstruction, segmentation and measurement consistency.
  • My role. Architecture, loss design, dataset organisation and the experimental protocol; hologram datasets are provided by a collaborator. Target for an initial manuscript: September 2026.
